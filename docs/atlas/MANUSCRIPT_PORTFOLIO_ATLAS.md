The key clinical message is practical. Ultrasound risk systems remain valuable for the question they were built to answer: which nodules are suspicious enough to biopsy or follow more closely. This study addresses a different question: once a cancer is already established, can the reported category be used as a surrogate for aggressive final pathology? Our results suggest that the answer is no, at least not in a way that should drive surgical extent or pathologic intensity by itself. For a patient in clinic, the safest interpretation is that the reported ultrasound category should remain one input among many. ...

## M106 — Comprehensive Mapping of Cervical Lymph Node Metastasis Patterns in Thyroid Carcinoma: A 25-Year Pathology-Based Analysis with Level-by-Level Histopathologic Correlation

**Target journal:** TBD (endocrine surgery / Thyroid)
**Status:** Internal Review (v1 draft completed 2026-05-15)

### Scope

- **Cohort / N:** 3,979 malignant thyroid carcinoma patients, 2000–2025; pathology LN cohort 3,904; any pathologic LNM 2,608; ENE-evaluable 2,592; strict skip denominator 323
- **Primary exposure:** Pathologic compartment (central, lateral, bilateral lateral) and cervical level (I–VII), pathology-only by hard rule
- **Primary outcome:** Level-by-level pathologic LNM; strict pathologic skip metastasis (lateral+, central evaluated, central=0) with explicit denominator; ENE-positive incidence among ENE-evaluable LN-positive patients (silence-by-omission rule for absence); nodal histology as a single verified column with retained provenance
- **Data sources:** Locked analytic table `thyroid-canonical-pub-2026.pub_workspace.m106_pathology_lnm_analytic_v1` (one row per patient, refreshed weekly Monday 06:00 UTC via stored procedure `pub_canonical.refresh_lnm_rebuild()`). Companion: `outputs/m106_final_data_readiness_lock_v2.md`. Manuscript tables workbook: `outputs/m106_manuscript_tables.xlsx`. Draft file: `m106_cervical_lnm_mapping/M106_cervical_LNM_mapping_v1_draft.docx`.

### What makes this manuscript distinct

Only manuscript that maps level-by-level cervical LNM across the full malignant thyroid carcinoma spectrum (PTC, microcarcinoma, FTC, MTC, PDTC, ATC, Hürthle) on a single denominator-defensible footing. Pathology-only by hard rule; imaging-derived LN fields explicitly excluded from primary endpoints. Introduces three reproducible analytic definitions: (1) strict pathologic skip metastasis with explicit denominator (lateral evaluable AND central examined); (2) ENE silence-by-omission rule for verified absence in synoptic + summary + comment + microscopic + gross narrative; (3) unified nodal histology column with retained provenance distinguishing pathology-text-extracted from primary-concordance-verified assignment.

### Methods excerpt

> This was a retrospective, single-institution, pathology-based cohort study of patients with malignant thyroid carcinoma who underwent surgical resection between January 1, 2000 and December 31, 2025. All primary endpoints, covariates, and denominators were drawn from a locked analytic table at the patient grain: pub_workspace.m106_pathology_lnm_analytic_v1. Construction is documented in the v2 data-readiness lock for this manuscript. Upstream sources were the institutional canonical patient master, the canonical surgical-pathology malignant-events table, the synoptic pathology table, and locked rollups for nodal histology (Scripts 280 and 280.1), level segments (Script 281), and ENE status (Script 282). A scheduled query refreshes the upstream rollups every Monday at 06:00 UTC. The pathology LN cohort was restricted to patients whose lymph-node rollup source was tumor_pathology or path_synoptics_fallback. Imaging-derived lymph node fields (ultrasound, CT, MRI, nuclear medicine, and clinical-note NLP) and imaging-side ENE fields were explicitly excluded from primary endpoint definitions; these were retained only as supplementary descriptive columns active solely for the small subset (n=2) of patients who did not have a pathology LN evaluation. Level-specific data for 137 patients with prior compartment-level inconsistency were re-parsed against the full synoptic pathology, pathology diagnosis summary, pathology diagnosis comment, and microscopic description using a structured large language model prompt that required mapping anatomic synonyms (paratracheal/pretracheal/delphian = level VI; mediastinal = level VII; high jugular = II; mid jugular = III; low jugular/supraclavicular = IV; posterior triangle = V; submental/submandibular = I) and prohibited duplicate splitting across listed levels. ENE was classified as positive (structured synoptic flag or explicit narrative positive wording), absent (explicit narrative negation OR no extranodal/extracapsular/perinodal wording anywhere = silence-by-omission), or indeterminate; restricted to LN-positive patients. Nodal histology was assigned via direct pathology-text extraction with primary-tumor concordance as verified fallback. Strict pathologic skip required lateral compartment positivity AND central compartment pathologically evaluated AND central compartment negative. Patient-level multivariable logistic regression evaluated demographic and primary-tumor predictors of five outcomes.

### Results excerpt

> The analytic cohort included 3,979 patients with malignant thyroid carcinoma; 3,904 (98.1%) had pathology lymph-node evaluation. Any pathologic LNM was identified in 2,608 patients (65.5% of analytic cohort, 66.8% of pathology LN cohort). Central compartment LNM was present in 862 patients (21.7%); lateral compartment LNM in 416 (10.5%); bilateral lateral LNM in 46 (1.2%). Among 323 patients eligible for strict skip analysis (lateral evaluable AND central pathologically examined), 32 (9.9%) had strict pathologic skip metastasis. The apparent-skip definition identified 168 patients (4.2%) as a sensitivity figure. Among 3,943 patients with usable level data after LLM remediation, level VI was involved in 794 (30.4% of LN-positive with usable level data), substantially above levels II–V (5.4–7.8%) and levels I and VII (0.1% and 0.4%). Among 2,608 LN-positive patients, 2,592 (99.4%) were ENE-evaluable: ENE positive in 1,112 (42.9%) and verified-absent in 1,480 (57.1%; 111 explicit narrative negation, 1,369 silence-by-omission). ENE-positive incidence was 55.3% in conventional PTC, 26.7% in PTC microcarcinoma, 8.3% in FTC, 69.4% in MTC, 35.0% in PDTC, and 46.7% in ATC. Nodal histology was assigned for 100% of LN-positive patients (1,008 by direct pathology text extraction; 1,600 verified by primary-tumor concordance). Multivariable logistic regression identified lymphatic invasion, extrathyroidal extension, and vascular invasion as the most consistent independent predictors across compartment and ENE endpoints; male sex was independently associated with any-LNM, central, lateral, and ENE-positive disease; FTC was associated with markedly lower central and lateral nodal positivity.

### Discussion / key findings

> The headline pattern is anatomic: level VI dominated the central compartment landscape and accounted for roughly four times the involvement seen at any individual lateral level. Levels II, III, IV, and V each showed cohort-level involvement in the 5–8% range and were broadly comparable. Levels I and VII were sparse across histologies and are best reported descriptively. Strict pathologic skip metastasis was uncommon (9.9% of the strict denominator; 0.8% of the overall cohort) — the denominator distinction matters because the apparent-skip definition (no requirement for central pathologic evaluation) identified roughly five times as many patients and is heavily influenced by clinical decisions about when to perform a central neck dissection. ENE was evaluable in 99.4% of LN-positive patients under a silence-by-omission rule that is consistent with how thyroid synoptic pathology is reported in practice. The histology-stratified ENE-positive incidence rates (PTC 55%, microcarcinoma 27%, FTC 8%, MTC 69%, PDTC 35%, ATC 47%) match the relative biological aggressiveness expected from the primary tumor pathology. Nodal histology, when reported as a single verified column rather than competing direct-text and inferred categories, is fully assignable for LN-positive thyroid carcinoma patients. The multivariable findings are clinically interpretable: lymphatic invasion, ETE, and vascular invasion are the most consistent independent predictors; male sex was associated with multiple nodal endpoints; FTC was associated with markedly lower nodal positivity in both compartments, consistent with the predominantly hematogenous biology of follicular carcinoma; MTC was associated with higher ENE-positive incidence, consistent with the recognized aggressive nodal phenotype of medullary carcinoma. Multifocality showed a counterintuitive inverse association with lateral positivity after adjustment, discussed cautiously as an exploratory finding.

### Conclusions

> In a 25-year, pathology-defined cohort of 3,979 malignant thyroid carcinoma patients, cervical lymph node metastasis is mappable at compartment and cervical-level resolution with explicit, denominator-defensible endpoints. Level VI dominates the central compartment landscape; levels II–V are individually less involved; levels I and VII are sparse. Strict pathologic skip metastasis is uncommon (9.9% of the strict denominator; 0.8% of cohort). With a silence-by-omission rule, ENE is evaluable in 99.4% of LN-positive patients and varies predictably with primary histology. Nodal histology is fully assignable as a single verified column with retained provenance. These patterns provide a level-resolved pathologic baseline against which preoperative imaging, molecular, and clinical-decision frameworks can be calibrated in future work.

### What makes this manuscript distinct

Pan-driver molecular exposure × granular multi-modal composite nodal phenotype. None of M006/M098/M099/M100/M106 maps the full molecular driver spectrum to compartment/level/ENE/burden simultaneously. M006 owns operative extent; M098 owns RAS-specific behavior; M099 owns comutation as primary; M100 owns fusion-only within-class biology; M106 owns pathology-only level-by-level mapping across all malignant carcinoma without molecular axis. M107 fills the niche of pan-driver molecular exposure × multi-modal composite nodal phenotype in the molecular-tested adult cohort and reports both crude and CORE-adjusted models with transparent detection-mediated attenuation analysis.

### Methods excerpt

> Retrospective observational cohort study using a locked BigQuery analytic layer (`pub_workspace.paperD_v2_*`, builder_version `paperD_v2_rebuild_2026-05-16_after_mig283b_mig285_mig287`) under STROBE, RECORD, and STREGA/STROBE-ME reporting principles. The cleaned primary cohort comprised 719 adult, operated, molecularly tested malignant thyroid carcinoma patients; pediatric (n=1), FTUMP-only (n=18), and follicular adenoma misclassified (n=1) were excluded. Patient-level molecular flags were rolled up by LOGICAL_OR across usable rows in canonical_molecular_genetics_v2 (excluding cancelled/inadequate). The mutually exclusive molecular driver group was assigned by hierarchy: BRAF V600E > BRAF non-V600E > RAS > RET-altered > Other fusion-driven > TERT/high-risk only > EIF1AX-only > No marker. For multivariable models the two smallest cells were collapsed into Other_high_risk_rare. Composite multi-source nodal endpoints unioned canonical_nodal_metastasis_resolved_v1, ln_master_rollup_v1 (deduped 1:1), canonical_path_malignant_events_v2, canonical_ln_level_counts_patient_rollup_v1, and clinical_note_ln_extracted_v1 (normalized); per-source flags were preserved for sensitivity. Unadjusted odds ratios used 2×2 tables with +0.5 continuity correction and Wald 95% CIs. Adjusted logistic regression was fit in BigQuery ML (LOGISTIC_REG, MLE, dummy encoding) at two adjustment levels: CORE (age + sex + histology group + tumor size + surgery year + ThyroSeq platform; primary) and Detection-adjusted (CORE + surgery type + central/lateral dissection + LN examined; secondary, used to expose detection-mediated attenuation). Nodal burden was modeled as a pooled Poisson rate ratio with log(LN examined) offset; BQ ML lacks native negative binomial regression, which is acknowledged as a methodological limitation. Sensitivity strata: DTC-only, PTC-only, preoperative/perioperative molecular, ThyroSeq-only, lateral-dissection-restricted, LN-positive-only for ENE, path-only endpoint definitions. Hierarchical multiplicity (ENE → lateral LNM → nodal burden → any LNM).

### Results excerpt

> The cleaned cohort (n=719) had median age 52, 75.9% female, median surgery year 2021, and median LN examined 1 (mean 4.4). Mutex molecular distribution: No marker 198 (27.5%), BRAF V600E 149 (20.7%), BRAF non-V600E 82 (11.4%), RAS 142 (19.7%), RET-altered 29 (4.0%), Other fusion 110 (15.3%), Other high-risk/rare 9 (1.3%). Composite endpoint event rates: any LNM 537/719 (74.7%), central LNM strict 105/719 (14.6%), lateral LNM 156/719 (21.7%), ENE any source 134/719 (18.6%), level V positive 32 (4.5%), strict skip 39 (5.4%), bilateral lateral 9 (1.3%). ENE prevalence varied 12-fold across molecular groups: RAS 5.6% vs RET-altered 55.2% (unadjusted OR 6.75, 95% CI 2.99–15.27); BRAF V600E 30.2% (OR 2.41, 1.43–4.04); BRAF non-V600E 25.6% (OR 1.93, 1.03–3.61). Lateral LNM was elevated in RET-altered (44.8%, OR 2.92, 1.32–6.47) and BRAF V600E (30.9%, OR 1.61, 0.99–2.60) and low in RAS (15.5%) and Other fusion (15.5%). Pooled Poisson IRR for nodal burden vs No marker: BRAF V600E 1.47 (1.21–1.79), RET-altered 1.75 (1.41–2.18), RAS 0.27 (0.14–0.49). After CORE adjustment for age, sex, histology, tumor size, surgery year, and platform, the RAS-protective ENE signal persisted (aOR 0.28) and the RET-altered burden signal persisted (IRR 1.75), but the BRAF V600E and RET-altered crude lateral signals attenuated substantially toward null (aOR 0.86 and 0.88), indicating histology- and detection-mediated mediation. Sensitivity strata (DTC-only, PTC-only, preop/periop, ThyroSeq-only) showed the same monotonic gradient. Path-only ENE was very sparse (3 No marker, 7 BRAF V600E, 4 RET-altered, 0 RAS).

### Discussion / key findings

> Three messages emerged. First, much of the unadjusted lateral and ENE gradient is histology- and detection-mediated; once histology and tumor size are held constant the residual molecular-only signal for lateral spread is modest. Second, RAS biology persists as the protective end of the spectrum across every sensitivity stratum (ENE aOR 0.28, IRR 0.27). Third, nodal burden tracks separately from spread pattern and remains strongly elevated for RET-altered and BRAF V600E groups after CORE adjustment. These findings position molecular driver class as one input — alongside cytology, ultrasound, tumor anatomy, and clinical examination — into individualized preoperative nodal-risk discussion, but do NOT support replacing existing clinical risk frameworks with a molecular-only algorithm, and do NOT support prophylactic lateral neck dissection driven by molecular class alone. Family guardrails are explicit: M006 owns operative extent; M098 owns RAS-specific behavior; M099 owns comutation primary; M100 owns within-fusion biology; M106 owns pathology-only level-by-level mapping; M107 owns pan-driver nodal phenotype. Limitations: composite multi-source endpoints are broader than path-only definitions; BQ ML logistic without Firth penalization for sparse cells (Other_high_risk_rare n=9 is descriptive only); nodal burden via pooled Poisson rate approximation rather than patient-level negative binomial; spatial transcriptomics linkage not available in exposed BQ data and removed from scope; selection bias toward indeterminate-Bethesda-driven testing patterns (Supplemental Table S6).

### Conclusions

> In a 719-patient adult operated molecularly tested thyroid carcinoma cohort drawn from a locked multi-modal institutional database, preoperative molecular driver class is strongly and biologically coherently associated with nodal metastatic phenotype. RAS marks the low-nodal-risk end of the spectrum; RET-altered and BRAF V600E mark the high end. After CORE adjustment, the RAS-protective and nodal-burden gradients persist while a substantial portion of the lateral-LNM and ENE crude signal is histology- and detection-mediated. These findings support using preoperative molecular class as one input into individualized nodal-risk discussion alongside cytology, imaging, and tumor anatomy, but do not support replacing existing clinical risk frameworks with a molecular-only algorithm.
