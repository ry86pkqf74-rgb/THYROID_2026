## 1. Themes relevant to this manuscript (non-quantitative)

### Molecular testing, Bethesda, and histopathology concordance

- **Clinical problem:** For **indeterminate** and **suspicious** cytology, molecular classifiers and panels are often used to refine malignancy risk and, in some pathways, surgical extent planning. **Concordance** between **preoperative molecular prediction** and **surgical pathology** is a standard **diagnostic accuracy** topic in the literature, typically reported with explicit gold standards, denominators, and often **confidence intervals** — none of which this folder’s **`table6_molecular_pathology_concordance.csv`** fully replaces without a prespecified diagnostic protocol.

- **Bethesda:** **Cytopathology tier** is a central preoperative risk stratifier. This study’s adjusted models already treat **Bethesda ≥4** as a key association with **initial total thyroidectomy**. Background literature should explain **why** higher Bethesda tiers shift toward more aggressive surgery in practice — as **context**, not as a result of this analysis.

- **Histopathology:** Final diagnosis (benign vs malignant; variant features) is the usual **outcome** for molecular performance papers. Here, the manuscript’s **`malignant_concordance_2x2`** frame is **descriptive** and based on **n = 20** preoperatively tested patients; it **must not** be rewritten as a rigorously powered accuracy study.

### Size-specific evidence gaps (2–4 cm)

- **Guideline and cohort heterogeneity:** Many publications address **1–4 cm** or **T2**-range disease; **2–4 cm**-specific data are often **subgroup** analyses with **wider CIs**. That pattern supports the **motivation** for an imaging-defined 2.0–4.0 cm cohort, but it does **not** justify overstating precision.

- **Extent choice vs oncologic outcomes:** Much of the debate in the literature contrasts **lobectomy vs total thyroidectomy** on **recurrence, completion surgery, RAI, survival** — endpoints **not** driving the v1 primary regression here (**`initial_total`** as outcome). Introduction/Discussion should **separate** (a) **practice-pattern / association** evidence (this paper) from (b) **long-term oncologic outcome** evidence (external sources).
