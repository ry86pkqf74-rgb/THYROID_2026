**2.4 Final pathologic diagnosis classification.** Final pathologic diagnosis was abstracted from the institutional surgical pathology synoptic registry, harmonized through the BigQuery canonical pathology layer (`thyroid-canonical-pub-2026.pub_canonical`). For each patient, primary diagnosis was drawn from `canonical_diagnosis_unified_v1` (combining `canonical_benign_diagnosis_v1` and `canonical_malignant_diagnosis_v1`); per-tumor histologic detail and AJCC8 staging were drawn from `canonical_path_malignant_events_v1`; indeterminate borderline diagnoses (NIFTP, FT-UMP, WDT-UMP) were drawn from `canonical_path_indeterminate_events_v1`; and granular benign diagnostic features were drawn from `canonical_path_benign_events_v1` patient-level rollup with `LOGICAL_OR` aggregation across multiple synoptic rows per patient. Histologic vocabulary was normalized via `canonical_histology_lookup_v1`.

Each patient was assigned to one of four mutually exclusive **pathology outcome classes**: (1) *pure benign*, no malignancy on final pathology; (2) *benign with incidental microcarcinoma*, dominant benign diagnosis with incidentally identified papillary thyroid microcarcinoma <1 cm; (3) *indeterminate*, NIFTP, FT-UMP, or WDT-UMP without frank malignancy; (4) *frank malignancy*, any malignant tumor ≥1 cm or any malignancy with adverse features (extrathyroidal extension, vascular invasion, nodal disease, or distant metastasis). Among patients with frank malignancy, dominant histology was assigned to one of eight **dominant malignant groups**: classical PTC, PTC variants (FV-PTC, tall-cell, columnar, others), follicular thyroid carcinoma, Hürthle/oncocytic carcinoma, medullary thyroid carcinoma, poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma, anaplastic thyroid carcinoma, and indeterminate borderline (collapsed across NIFTP/FT-UMP/WDT-UMP).

We additionally enumerated all 30 individual benign histologic entities defined by the institutional synoptic dictionary (multinodular goiter and substernal multinodular goiter were collapsed into the cohort definition; remaining 28 entities reported in Table 8). NLP-derived flags were validated against an investigator-curated hand-count over the same source records: agreement at the patient level was {{N}}/30 categories with exact concordance, with residual {{≤k}}-patient variance attributable to annotator-equivocal markers and free-text vs binary documentation conventions; two categories (atypical adenoma and substernal multinodular goiter) underwent additional LLM-assisted adjudication of source synoptic text by Claude (Anthropic) and were reconciled via additive override rows in `canonical_path_benign_overrides_v1` (audit anchors: MFL-20260507-H2-PATHEXPAND, DFL-20260507-H2-BINARIZATION-CORRECTION, VC-H2-ATYPICAL-ADENOMA-NLP-ANOMALY, VC-H2-SUBSTERNAL-PATH-RECONCILE).

**Two-layer cohort pathology NLP architecture (H2 v3.2 Phase 2b).** BigQuery exposes a frozen **raw NLP** cohort view, `pub_workspace.cohort_h2_pathology_outcome_v1`, which preserves machine-extracted boolean flags exactly as produced in the NLP pipeline for audit and replication. Analytic manuscripts use **`pub_workspace.cohort_h2_pathology_outcome_v2`**, which is row-aligned with v1 (N={{6075}}, 64 columns) but replaces two flags—`nlp_atypical_adenoma` and `nlp_thymic_tissue`—with values corrected by left join to `pub_canonical.canonical_path_benign_overrides_v1` (`COALESCE(override_flag, raw_nlp_flag)`). On 2026-05-08 the override ledger contained **17** rows affecting those two categories: **11** `atypical_adenoma` NLP false-positive reversals, **4** `thymic_tissue` NLP false-positive reversals, and **2** `thymic_tissue` NLP false-negative augmentations (manual-positive cases the NLP layer had missed). After correction, manuscript-facing counts are **atypical adenoma n=7** and **thymic tissue n=252** (vs raw NLP n=18 and n=254 in v1). Phase 3 SQL templates and analytic jobs **must `FROM` v2** for these two entities; v1 remains the traceable raw-NLP stratum.

For statistical comparisons, race-stratified frequencies of pathology outcome classes, dominant malignant groups, and individual benign categories were tested with chi-square (or Fisher exact for sparse cells, defined as any race-group cell <5). Pairwise contrasts (Black or African American vs White, Asian vs White, Other vs White) were computed with Bonferroni correction and Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control across the full family of tests, with White race as the reference group consistent with §3.7. Adjusted models (logistic for binary frank-malignancy outcome, multinomial for the four-level outcome class) included age at surgery, sex, body mass index, smoking status, diabetes, hypertension, prior radiation exposure, family history of thyroid disease, prior cancer history, Bethesda category (restricted to patients with at least one preoperative FNA, n={{≈2,922}}), molecular testing receipt, gland weight, and any substernal extension; sensitivity analyses used multiple imputation for body mass index and Bethesda category. Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit, Brier score, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve are reported for binary models.
